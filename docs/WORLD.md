# The Void — World Record

_The authoritative worldbuilding record, from the 2026-08-25 interview. Before this document the
entire repository contained **one paragraph** of authored world prose; the lore file read "this is a
lore this is a lore". Everything here is the author's, decided deliberately._

**Read this before writing any narration, any prose, or any art prompt.** Companions:
`docs/GAME-DESIGN.md` (systems), `docs/ART-BIBLE.md` (how it looks), `docs/UI-DESIGN.md` (how it
reads on screen).

---

## 0. The spine — one axis

> **The condition is the Hollow.**

That single line resolves the whole game. The Void is not a place you travel to; it is what happens
to you, and what happens to you is **hollowing**. Everything else is that axis seen from a different
angle:

| | |
|---|---|
| The descent | being hollowed out, stage by stage |
| The **Hollowed** (floor 5 family) | people the condition finished |
| Your **Hollow Self** (final boss) | the condition wearing your face |
| The **Hollow** class | playing as the condition, openly, without the pretence |
| The narrator's voice | the hollowing itself, speaking |
| The grace ending — *"made whole"* | **the exact opposite of hollow** |

The good ending does not say *made well*. It says **made whole**. That is not decoration; it is the
win condition of the entire game stated in one word. Whole is what hollow is not.

**Consequence for every writer, human or model:** never use "hollow" casually. It is the game's
load-bearing word. Reserve it.

---

## 1. Absolution — the last city **[LOCKED]**

The capital of Absolution, 2100. **It is the last city.** Nothing viable exists outside the walls;
every living person is inside them. There is no elsewhere to be exiled *to*.

This does real work:
- **The Undercity is the only "away" that exists.** Sending someone down is not relocation, it is
  the only form of banishment available in a world with nowhere else.
- **The Rift is the last frontier.** In a closed world, the only unexplored direction is down.
- **The name reads as desperation, not piety.** Absolution or nothing.

## 2. The houses — noble-magical dynasties **[LOCKED]**

Power is held by **bloodlines**, not corporations. Magic runs in families and always has; the houses
are aristocracy who also happen to run the economy. **House Grandmore** is one of them.

- An **Arch-Mage** is the head of a family, not a CEO.
- **Memorians** are sworn hereditary retainers — an office passed down, not a job posting.
- Obligation runs on **oath and blood-debt**, not contract. **Refusing is dishonour, not
  resignation.** This is feudal logic wearing a 2100 skin.

### Magic and technology — no line at all **[LOCKED]**

**Magic was industrialised.** What was once ritual is now infrastructure. A warding sigil and a
firewall are the same department; a shock baton and a lightning spell are the same catalogue at
different price points. The engine's element list — Physical, Cryo, Pyro, Electro, Poison, Psychic,
Force — is a list of **utilities**, and nobody in-world finds that strange.

**This is why the techno-occult look is not a style choice — it is simply accurate.** Riot plate
with a hand-scratched ward is not a juxtaposition to anyone living there. It is just equipment.

**The Void is the only thing in this world that is not understood.**

## 3. The Rift — unknown, and contested **[LOCKED]**

Nobody knows where it came from. It has been there for all of living memory, every explanation is
disputed, and **the houses each maintain the history that keeps them in charge** — three houses,
three incompatible accounts, each doctrine serving whoever teaches it.

No answer is available in-world. The player's theory is as good as anyone's, and the narrator can
never be pinned down on it.

> **Note the deliberate split:** the houses do not know what the Rift *is* (§3) but they know
> exactly what the descent *does* (§5). Origin is contested; consequence is documented.

## 4. The mission — and the moment it stops being one **[LOCKED]**

**The game has two premises, not one.**

**Floor 1 is a job.** The Kingpins of the Undercity moved on their plan; the Arch-Mage of house
Grandmore ordered you down to kill them and bring back their brainchips. Floor 1 is the last real
place and this is a real errand with a real target.

**After floor 1 there is no mission.** You kill the Kingpin, someone throws you into the Rift, and
from that moment the game is **escape and survival**. Not a new quest — the *absence* of one. Nobody
is waiting for a report. Nothing you do down there serves anyone above.

**Every narrator and every piece of prose must respect this break.** Floor 1 can talk about
objectives. Floors 2–5 must not. The silence where the mission used to be is the point.

### Brainchips — a life, recorded **[LOCKED]**

A brainchip holds **everything a person experienced** — what they saw, said, wanted. Extraction
means the Memorians read *all* of it, not merely the part anyone asked about.

So the order — *"return with their brainchips so that Grandmore's Memorians can extract all the
available information"* — is quietly obscene, and **the houses do this routinely and have for
generations.** Your errand is a violation dressed as an errand. This is live material for the karma
system, and it means the Neuromancer's own office is the one that performs the violation.

## 5. What the houses know **[LOCKED]**

**They know exactly.** The floors are named in their archives. Memorians debrief what returns. The
descent has been documented, studied and managed by these bloodlines for generations.

**The Arch-Mage knew precisely what he was ordering you into.** The mission is a knowing betrayal
from its first line.

> **The most important structural idea in this document.** The Void is *subjective* (§6) — and the
> houses hold *accurate institutional records* of it. They know the stages because every descent
> reports the same stages. It is a **clinical literature, generations deep.** There is a document
> upstairs that explains what is happening inside you, it is correct, and **it does not help you at
> all.** That is the psychosis origin rendered as worldbuilding rather than as metaphor — the file
> is right and the file is useless, and someone above is reading your descent.

## 6. The Void — a condition, not a location **[LOCKED]**

**The Void is not a place.** It is what happens to a mind that goes far enough down. The floors are
**stages, not levels**. The descent is entirely real to the person having it and **unverifiable to
anyone else** — "where did you go" has no answer.

And per §0: **the condition is the Hollow.** Descending is being hollowed.

### There is no way back up **[LOCKED]**

The route closes behind you, every floor. You cannot climb out. **The only exit is the floor-4
verdict** — which is why nobody simply turns around, why the Warden is a literal door, and why floor
5 is what lies past the door you failed. Down is the only direction that leads out.

### The stages **[LOCKED]**

| Floor | Stage | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — The Undercity** | *before* | The last real place. Still solid, still literal, still a world with a job in it |
| **2 — Entrance to the Void** | **FRACTURE** | The mind coming apart into pieces that argue with each other. Reflections, Mirror-Selves, doubles — *your own pieces, disagreeing* |
| **3 — The Ash City** | **GRIEF** | The ash is **what you lost**. The Feelings and the Sins are things you are mourning. Endless, silent, exhausting |
| **4 — The Angelic Underground** | **JUDGEMENT** | And something **real** is weighing it — see below |
| **5 — The True Void** | **ABSENCE** | Not dissolution. The discovery that **there was less of you than you thought** |

**Floor 3 is grief, not fear** — that reframing is load-bearing. The Ash City's horror is mourning
and exhaustion, not terror. The ash is the residue of loss, which is why nothing burns: *the fire is
already out.*

**Floor 5 is absence, not destruction.** You are not torn apart; you find out how little was there.
That is what the Unmade, the Echoes and the Hollowed are all saying in different ways.

### Floor 4 — the angels are REAL **[LOCKED]**

**They exist.** The condition does not manufacture them — it removes whatever normally stops you
perceiving them. Going down is what lets you *see*.

**This is the most important decision in the whole record.** It means:
- **The descent is not purely delusion.** Something true is down there.
- **Grace is genuine mercy from something real**, not a story you told yourself.
- **Floor 4 is the one floor that is not a lie** — every other stage distorts; this one reveals.
- The houses' archives would know this too, which is its own quiet horror.

In a game built out of psychosis, this is the difference between a horror story and a **survivor's**
story — and the author's own word for himself is *survivor*, not sufferer.

## 7. You **[LOCKED]**

**A sworn retainer of house Grandmore.** You belong to the house — by oath or by blood-debt — and
this is what you are *for*. Refusing was never available to you.

*"You are the last hope of your society"* was always a leash.

### The five classes — household offices **[LOCKED]**

Four are real posts within Grandmore. Every one of them already knows the others.

| Class | Office |
|---|---|
| **Enforcer** | The house's muscle and its law |
| **Neuromancer** | **A Memorian** — the memory-readers who extract brainchips. This is why they fight with the mind, and **why one of them was sent to recover a chip.** The office and the mission are the same thing |
| **Scavver** | The house's salvager and fixer; works the Undercity, knows its routes |
| **Penitent** | Its confessor and oath-keeper. Pays in blood, which is exactly how the class plays |
| **Hollow** | **Not an office.** What is left of a retainer with no house. The anti-role: unbound |

The Neuromancer link is the neatest thing in the record — it makes the mission's own premise
internally motivated, and it means the class that performs the violation of §4 is a playable one.

### The Hollow — it was never separate from you **[LOCKED]**

Your Hollow Self is not a copy, a fate, or a thing the Void made. **It is the part of you that was
always there.** Beating it means *acknowledging* it.

So playing as the Hollow is **playing without the pretence** — and the hidden karma system has known
all along. The unlock is not a menu item; it is a confession.

## 8. The narrator **[LOCKED]**

**The narrator is the Hollow** — the condition itself, given a voice. Not a character. Not an entity
with plans. The hollowing, speaking.

This settles how it must be written:
- **Second person**, because it is inside you, not beside you.
- **It never answers questions**, because there is nobody there to answer them.
- **It is unreliable without ever intending to deceive** — it is not lying to you or telling you the
  truth, it is what it sounds like in there.
- The existing persona line in `src/llm/narrate.ts` — *"vivid, terse, concrete and unsettling"* —
  stays exactly right. **"Concrete and unsettling" governs the art as much as the prose.**

## 9. The endings **[LOCKED]**

**Grace / ASCENSION — you come back, and you are changed.** You return to Absolution as someone who
went down and came up. Not restored to who you were: **integrated**. The descent becomes something
you *carry* rather than something you escaped.

**"Made whole" ≠ "made well."** Survivor, not cured. See §0 — whole is the opposite of hollow, and
that is the whole game.

**Damnation — cast down, and the Void claims its own.** You failed the only door out (§6) and floor
5 is what is past it. Absence, then.

## 10. Still open

- **The Kingpins.** Named as the mission's target and the last purely human enemy, but nothing says
  who they are, how many, or what their "convoluted plan" was.
- **The rival houses.** Three contested histories of the Rift implies at least three houses;
  only Grandmore exists.
- **What a descent looks like from above** — how someone is put into the Rift, and by whom.
- **Absolution itself** as a place, above the Undercity. Only the flooded industrial underside is
  described (`ART-BIBLE.md` §4).
- **The Void's own name.** Who started calling it that, and whether the houses use a different word
  in their archives.
